## Failures

### F-1 (required check, Amendment A item 4): under a sustained stream of urgent-tier detection speech an alert is never heard to the end; the delay is unbounded

- Steps (about 1 minute): `$PY -m pytest tests/uat/test_t00a_alert_scheduler.py -k "urgent_detections_that_leave_gaps or (cut_each_other and 0.1)" --runxfail -q -p no:cacheprovider` (`evidence/2026-10-18-tester-finding-F1-repro.log`). The tests start the real `tts_worker` with a stub espeak, offer an urgent-tier detection item (1.2 s phrase) every 1.5 s (or 0.8, 0.3, 0.1 s), raise an alert, and wait 8 s for it to be spoken to the end.
- Expected (Amendment A item 4): raised -> spoken at most about 3 s. Actual: **never within 8 s, 12 of 12 trials** in both environments. With normal-tier detections every 0.1 s the same measurement gives median 0.46 s and max 1.27 s, so only the urgent tier is affected.
- Why (read from the code and the stub log):
  1. At a detection period of 0.1 to 0.8 s the speech worker is never idle (urgent items cut each other, as they do today), `alerts._busy` is never clear, so the scheduler **never offers the alert** (`offers: []` in the debug run).
  2. At a period of 1.5 s there is a gap after each phrase; the alert is offered in it (offers at 0.30, 1.80, 3.31 s ...) and taken, but the next urgent detection arrives while it speaks and `priority.preempts()` says an urgent-tier item preempts anything, so the alert is **cut after 0.18 s of its 0.3 s** every time. It is re-spoken after the next phrase and cut again. Real alert wording is longer than the stub's 0.3 s, so it is cut earlier still. Brief criterion 6 accepts a cut ("spoken again as soon as the channel is free"), but there is no bound on the repetition.
- Where this can happen in the product (read, not run): `pipeline/callbacks.py:548-552` lets an urgent-tier object bypass the 10 s "still there" floor, so a vehicle or motorcycle that stays in the centre of the view gets a new urgent item offered at the callback's frame rate. I did not run the pipeline (no hardware needed or used).
- Comparison with the baseline (`evidence/2026-10-18-tester-baseline-announce-under-urgent-stream.log`, baseline `tts_worker`): a mode announcement of 2.0 s is **also cut by the next urgent detection** (period 1.5 s or 0.1 s) and, being one-shot, is lost. So the cut itself is today's `preempts()` rule (`core/priority.py`, outside this task's scope), not a regression. What T-00a adds: the alert waits for the phrase in progress (Amendment A item 4 accepted this) and, with a short alert in a 1.5 s stream, the baseline announcement completes (it cuts the phrase at once) where the T-00a alert does not. A fix that only drops the busy gate would not be enough, because the next urgent item would cut the alert again.
- For the Lead to decide (I do not): (a) accept it as a known limit and ask the user (any real fix changes who may preempt whom, and the brief says preemption must behave as today); (b) have the speech worker not let a detection item preempt an alert utterance in progress (only another announcement may), about three lines in `_speak_interruptible`, plus a gate so the alert is not starved by a never-idle worker; (c) leave to T-01a to see whether it matters in practice. The 4 tests are `xfail(strict=True)`: when it is fixed they turn into failures that say "remove the marker".
