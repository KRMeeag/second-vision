# F5 · Camera blocked, too dark or too bright

> **Update 2026-10-19 (real footage):** on the device a finger over the lens and a phone flash were both missed. The
> rules were changed; see "Real footage and the changed rules" at the end. The thresholds below in "What the wearer
> gets" are the first ones; the current ones are at the end.

2026-10-19, one session (Opus). The code and the unit and real-GStreamer tests are done. **Not done: the
performance run on the device and the device check** (cover the lens, shine a torch); both need the boot service
stopped. No git writes.

## What the wearer gets

| What the camera sees (3 checks in a row, about 3 s) | Spoken (every 30 s while it lasts) |
|---|---|
| Blocked or too dark: 95th-percentile brightness under 40, or mean under 14 (0 to 255) | "Camera is blocked or it is too dark. Obstacle warnings may be missing." |
| Too bright: mean over 240 | "Camera is facing very bright light. Obstacle warnings may be missing." |
| Usable again (3 good checks in a row) | "Camera view is back." once |

These are failsafe alerts like the others (D41): spoken with the speech switch off. No vibration (none was asked for).

## Where the frame comes from, and the checks against the Hailo docs (AGENTS.md Rule 6)

- **The place.** Every mode builds on hailo_apps' `SOURCE_PIPELINE`, which ends in `capsfilter name=source_fps_caps`
  with caps `video/x-raw, format=RGB` at the app's width and height (read in the installed library,
  `gstreamer_helper_pipelines.py`, lines 95 to 230). All four builders (`_build_dual`, `_build_detection_only`,
  `_build_depth_only`, `_build_idle`) pass through it before any inference. So the check sees the camera's own frames.
- **Reading the frame.** hailo_apps' documented pair: `get_caps_from_pad(pad)` and
  `get_numpy_from_buffer(buffer, format, width, height)`, which returns an RGB `(H, W, 3)` copy. Checked in the
  workbench docs (`.hailo/toolsets/core-framework-api.md`, `buffer_utils` section; `.hailo/memory/common_pitfalls.md`:
  frames are RGB) and in the installed `buffer_utils.py` (it maps, copies and unmaps). The test stand-ins already provide both.
- **Why a one-shot probe, not one on every frame.** A Python probe on every frame makes the camera's streaming
  thread take Python's lock (the GIL) 30 times a second, behind the depth callback, which holds it for about 15 ms
  per frame. The one-shot probe runs Python once a second; between checks that thread runs no Python at all.
  `Gst.PadProbeReturn.REMOVE` is not used anywhere in hailo-apps or this repo, so I did not take it on trust: a
  real-GStreamer test on this Pi (GStreamer 1.26.2) shows one frame taken per tick, the probe gone after it, and the
  stream at 30 fps. Adding a probe is not a state change. `app.py` already adds and removes a buffer probe when it
  primes the hailonets during a start; here the probe is added while the pipeline plays, which the same test exercises.
- **The timer** runs on the library's main loop, which is a plain `GLib.MainLoop()` on the default context
  (`gstreamer_app.py`, line 473), so a timer added in `run()` before `super().run()` fires.

## Decisions I made (please confirm or overrule)

1. **Both rockers off (mode "none"): no check, and any view alert is withdrawn.** There are no obstacle warnings to
   miss, so "Obstacle warnings may be missing" would only be noise.
2. **A blocked view is not a "missing part".** The camera is connected, so it does not hold back "Everything is
   connected" when another part returns.
3. **Straight from dark to bright (or back)** says the new sentence, not "Camera view is back".
4. **Camera lost:** the view alerts are withdrawn silently and the camera's own alert speaks (the spec's "not while
   the camera is missing"). When the camera returns, the check starts afresh: three checks again.
5. **The thresholds are the agreed placeholders.** URGENT-TESTS.md says they come from real footage; none has been
   taken. Every change in what the check sees is logged with the measured mean and 95th percentile
   (`[VIEW] the camera looks dark (mean 6.1, 95th percentile 11.0)`), so the device check gives the numbers.

## Tested

New file `tests/test_camera_view.py`, **50 tests** (47 in Poetry; the 3 real-GStreamer ones run in `my_hailo_env`).

- **The check on synthetic frames:** black, a very dark room, a hand with light leaking, a normal room, a plain grey
  wall, a white wall in daylight, a dark room with a lamp filling a tenth of the view (not flagged), bright, pure white,
  noise; a lamp too small to light the room (flagged dark, as the rule says); the exact boundaries (95th percentile 40,
  mean 14 on its own, mean 240); every frame format hailo_apps hands out (RGB, YUYV, NV12, grey); the grey weights.
- **Three in a row, on a virtual clock** (the real scheduler and a speech model): raised at the third check and said
  every 30 s; two are not enough; an alternating scene never flaps; three good checks say "back" once, two do not;
  dark straight to bright; bright then back; dark and back twice; `reset()` silent and fresh; a blocked view does not
  hold back "Everything is connected"; a scheduler error retried; checks and resets from four threads at once.
- **The app's frame request, GStreamer replaced by fakes:** one request per tick and never stacked (the waiting one is
  left alone); it takes one frame and removes itself; three dark frames raise, three good ones clear; nothing while the
  camera is lost, waiting or starting; a frame arriving after the camera failed is not judged; mode "none"; a pipeline
  without the source element; a request left on a replaced pipeline is taken off; a failing frame is logged and the probe
  still goes; the error log does not flood; the timer stops with the program; no scheduler, no request; a camera loss
  withdraws the view alert and its own alert follows; `run()` starts the timer only when there is a scheduler.
- **Real GStreamer** (`videotestsrc` switched between black, white and a test card, 30 fps): dark, then bright, then
  "back"; **9 ticks, 9 frames checked**; the stream at **30.3 fps**; no errors. A stream with no frames (paused) gets no
  stacked requests, and a rebuild takes the waiting request off the old pipeline.
- **Cost:** one check on a real 640x480 RGB buffer, median **1.03 ms** (max 2.0 ms), which is **0.10 % of one core** at
  one a second, against the 1 % limit. Of that, the grey sample and the decision are 0.14 ms; the rest is the library's
  frame copy.
- **Mutation check:** 24 changes, each removing one behaviour in a scratch copy, run in `my_hailo_env`
  (`evidence/F5-mutations.txt`). The first run left one survivor: removing the "do not stack requests" guard went
  unnoticed, because the replaced-request path then swapped the waiting probe for a new one. The test now also checks
  that a waiting request is left alone, and **all 24 are caught**.
- **Repeats:** the new file 10 times in `my_hailo_env` and 10 in Poetry, all passed (`evidence/F5-repeat-10runs.txt`).
- **Full suites** (each is the previous total plus the new tests, 0 failed):
  - Poetry: 982 passed, 216 skipped (`evidence/F5-poetry-full.txt`);
  - `my_hailo_env`: 1081 passed, 117 skipped (`evidence/F5-hailo-env-full.txt`).

## Not tested, and known limits

- **The performance run on the device** (the spec's "must not lag" acceptance). Measured off the device only: the cost
  above, and 30 fps through a real pipeline. On the device, once a second the camera's streaming thread holds the GIL
  for about 1 ms, and may first wait for it behind the depth callback (about 15 ms). That is at most one late frame a
  second; the run below shows whether it can be seen.
- **Real footage.** The thresholds are unverified on this camera (an Arducam IMX291, low-light model, which may lift a
  dark room's brightness). A torch may not reach "mean over 240" unless it fills the view.
- **A frozen camera that sends no frames but reports no error** gets no checks, so no view alert. The camera-loss
  handling covers cameras that fail with an error, not one that silently stops.
- The first check comes 1 s after start: a lens covered at start-up is reported about 3 s after the camera starts.

## Real footage and the changed rules (2026-10-19)

The user ran the program on the device (camera unplug and the on-screen status both worked) and sent two screenshots of
the cv2 window that the check had called "ok": a finger over the lens, and a phone flash into it. Measured on the picture
area of those screenshots (overlay lines painted over; kept as `tests/data/camera-view-finger.png` and
`tests/data/camera-view-phone-flash.png`):

| Frame | First rules saw | Why they missed it | New rules see |
|---|---|---|---|
| Finger | mean 19.7, 95th percentile of single pixels **54** | the low-light camera raises its gain: coloured noise (red 46, green 6, blue 12) whose speckles lift the 95th percentile over 40 | 16x16 **block averages**: mean 19.1, 95th percentile **27.9** (under 40): **dark** |
| Phone flash | mean **217** (rule: over 240) | this camera's white tops out at about **241** (brightness), so "mean over 240" could never fire | **62 %** of the blocks at white (230 or more; rule: 40 % or more): **bright** |

**The rules now** (`core/camera_view.py`): the sample is the average of each 16x16 block (1,200 values), not every 16th
pixel. Dark: the blocks' 95th percentile under 40 or their mean under 14 (the agreed numbers, unchanged). Too bright: at
least 40 % of the blocks at white (230 or more), replacing "mean over 240". Three checks in a row, as before.

**Still to check on the device (the user):** a dim room and a normal room must stay clear. The screen now shows the
measured numbers live, top right (`VIEW avg 118  top 5% 214  white 3%`); `SV_VIEW_LOG=1` also prints every check to the
terminal. If a dim room is flagged, read its "top 5%" and lower `DARK_P95_BELOW` below it; if a bright window is
flagged, raise `BRIGHT_WHITE_SHARE`.

**Tests changed** (deliberately, they pinned the first rule): the agreed-numbers test, the boundaries test (now also the
40 % white boundary), and one status-display test (the measurements moved from under the banner to the live corner).
**New:** the two real frames (and why the first rules missed them), block averages cancelling noise, the numpy fallback
matching OpenCV, odd frame sizes, a bright window filling a third (clear) or more than half (bright) of the view, the
opt-in per-check log, the live readout.

**Verified:** mutation check of the changed rules, 8 of 8 caught (`evidence/F5-real-mutations.txt`: single pixels instead of
block averages, the old mean rule back, white at 245, 70 % needed, a non-inclusive white boundary, a wrong numpy fallback,
no live readout, the per-check log always on). Full suites, 0 failed: Poetry 1031 passed, 216 skipped
(`evidence/F5-real-poetry-full.txt`); `my_hailo_env` 1130 passed, 117 skipped (`evidence/F5-real-hailo-env-full.txt`).
