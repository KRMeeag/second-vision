## What changed

| File | Change |
|---|---|
| `src/second_vision/core/alerts.py` | `raise_alert(..., on_speak=callable)`. The scheduler calls it each time the speech worker **starts** saying that alert: at once, on every repeat, and again if an utterance was cut and is said anew. It runs on the speech worker's thread **after the scheduler's lock is released** (so it may call the scheduler), and an error in it is logged and changes nothing. The default is `None`, so every existing caller is unchanged. Docstring updated. |
| `src/second_vision/pipeline/app.py` | `_raise_camera_missing` raises the missing-camera alert with `on_speak = patterns.play(PATTERN_CAMERA)` (F1's longer *beep beep*). Without a pattern player on `user_data` the sentence is said as before. One new import. |

What the wearer gets:

| Event | Spoken | Felt |
|---|---|---|
| Camera lost, or absent at start-up | "Critical Error: Camera cannot be reached" at once, then every 10 s (F0) | The camera *beep beep* (two long pulses) **at the start of each sentence**, so every 10 s |
| Camera back | "Camera has been reconnected" once (F0) | Nothing (REQUIREMENTS section 8: no vibration for the camera's return) |

**Why a hook, not a timer.** DEMO-TRACK asks for the pattern "each time the alert is spoken". The camera code
only runs when the camera is lost, not every 10 s, and a timer of its own would drift from the speech (the
scheduler starts the next sentence 10 s after the *start* of the last one, and waits while the speech channel
is busy). Tying the pattern to the utterance start keeps what is felt and what is heard together.

## Behaviour to know

- **A sentence that is cut and said again** (a mode announcement cuts it) asks for the pattern again. The
  pattern player keeps at most one of each waiting, so cuts never pile patterns up.
- **Earphones out:** the sentence goes into nothing, but the pattern still comes. The wearer is not left with
  no sign of a camera fault.
- **Glasses board out:** patterns are dropped (F1), so only the sentence remains.
- **Strength and the vibration switch:** a failsafe pattern plays with the switch off, at max(knob, 30 %)
  (F1, decision D41). F4 changes nothing there.
- **The depth vibration** stops with the camera anyway, so the pattern has the motors to itself.

## Tested

New file `tests/test_camera_alert_vibration.py`, **20 tests**.

- **The hook on a virtual clock** (the real scheduler and speech model):
  - fires when each utterance starts, not when the alert is raised, and stops with the alert;
  - an alert without a hook calls nothing and is not disturbed; a silent alert never calls it;
  - an error in the hook never stops the speech or the scheduler;
  - the hook is called outside the scheduler's lock (a hook that asks the scheduler something would otherwise
    hang; the test runs in a thread with a deadline, because `pytest-timeout` is not installed in the Poetry
    environment);
  - a cut sentence asks again; raising the same alert again replaces the hook.
- **The real camera methods** (`unit_app` of `tests/test_camera_alerts.py`, GStreamer replaced, a recorder in
  place of the pattern player):
  - lost while running: the pattern at 5, 15, 25, 35 s, the same instants as the sentence;
  - absent at start-up: at 0, 10, 20, 30 s;
  - the return stops it and gives none of its own; a start that fails keeps one pattern per sentence;
  - lost twice; no pattern player; a pattern player that raises.
- **The real speech worker on a stub espeak-ng**, speech switch on and off: the hook fires within a few
  milliseconds of the start of each real utterance, and the sentence is spoken with the switch off (D41).
- **Mutation check:** 9 changes, each removing one behaviour in a scratch copy (hook never called; called under
  the lock; an error in it propagates; fired when raised instead of when spoken; re-raise keeps the old hook;
  camera alert without the pattern; the wrong pattern; camera code needing a pattern player; a pattern on the
  reconnect sentence). **All 9 made a test fail** (`evidence/F4-mutations.txt`). The lock mutation first
  *hung* its test instead of failing it; the test was rewritten to be bounded, and now fails in 10 s.
- **Repeats:** F4's and F6's files 10 times in a row, all passed (`evidence/F4-F6-repeat-10runs.txt`).
- **Full suites after F4 and F6:** see "Full suites" at the end of `F6.md`.

## Not tested, and known limits

- **Nothing on the device yet.** Whether the camera pattern is felt at the 30 % floor, and whether it can be told from the reconnect one, is F1's open question.
- **The real 10 s cadence with the real camera** is in the device check.
- The pattern starts when the speech worker takes the sentence. The sentence itself starts a few ms later
  (the stub test measures up to 0.5 s of slack, mostly process start).

## Device check (batched session)

The boot service must be stopped first; the user stops it or approves it. Run the program from this worktree
(see `DEMO-CHECKLIST.md`, Part B). Then:

1. `sudo -n .claude/tools/usb_sim.py detach camera --for 40`.
   - Expect "Critical Error: Camera cannot be reached" at once and every 10 s, **each with two long pulses**
     on all three motors, starting with the sentence.
2. The camera returns: "Camera has been reconnected", **no pulse**.
3. Vibration switch **off**, again: the pulses still come.
4. The glasses board out, camera out: sentences only, no pulses; board back: the reconnect *beep beep*
   (F2) is shorter than the camera one. Tell them apart.
5. Write in `F4.md` what was felt. The log line is `[PATTERN] playing 'camera'`.
